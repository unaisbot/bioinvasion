What is Biological Invasion?

A biological invasion occurs when species are introduced to new environments, establish themselves, and spread uncontrollably, causing significant harm to biodiversity.

Pathways

Biological invasions often begin through human-mediated pathways such as global trade, transport, agriculture, and ornamental horticulture. Species may be introduced intentionally or accidentally and later escape into natural ecosystems, where they establish and spread.

Consequences

Once established, invasive species can outcompete native flora and fauna, alter ecosystem processes, and reduce biodiversity. Their impacts extend beyond ecology, affecting livelihoods, food security, water resources, and local economies.

Management

Effective management of biological invasion relies on prevention, early detection, and rapid response. Combining scientific research, policy support, and community participation is essential to control invasive species and safeguard ecosystem resilience.

Species of the week

Wedelia trilobata

Wedelia trilobata, commonly known as Singapore daisy, is a fast-spreading invasive groundcover native to Central America. It forms dense mats that suppress native plants, alters soil conditions, and rapidly invades gardens, wetlands, and forest edges, posing a serious threat to local biodiversity.
